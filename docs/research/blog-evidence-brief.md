## How to cite in a Kaja note

Good: “The US State Department’s language school puts Korean with Arabic, Chinese, and Japanese: about 2,200 classroom hours to professional working proficiency.”

## 2. `korean-conversation-practice`

**Draft claim:** conversation ≠ shadowing; needs another person (timing, repair); language exchange is full of flirting; shy people need a tutor; one live 20-minute talk a week.

| Source | What it actually says | Use in the post |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|

| Swain, M. (1985). Communicative competence: Some roles of comprehensible input and output. In *Input in Second Language Acquisition*. | Output **pushes** learners from understanding to encoding. Noticing gaps, hypothesis testing. | Shadowing is not conversation: no interlocutor, no repair. |
| Gass, S. & Mackey, A. (2006/2007). Input, interaction and output: An overview. *AILA Review*. | Feedback in interaction modifies input **and** pushed output. | Tutor vs HelloTalk: the useful bit is interruption + reformulation. |
| MacIntyre, P. D., Dörnyei, Z., Clément, R., & Noels, K. A. (1998). Conceptualizing willingness to communicate in a L2. *The Modern Language Journal*, 82, 545–562. | WTC = readiness to talk **at a particular time with a particular person**. Anxiety + perceived competence sit right under it. | Shy ≠ lazy. A paid tutor is a designed low-threat slot, not magic. |
| Horwitz, E. K., Horwitz, M. B., & Cope, J. (1986). Foreign language classroom anxiety. *The Modern Language Journal*, 70, 125–132. | FLCAS: speaking anxiety is a stable, measurable barrier. | Don’t moralize freeze-ups. |

**Caveat:** There is no RCT that “italki 20 min/week beats HelloTalk.” Cite interaction/output, then your seven years of HelloTalk as the field note.

**Line you can add:** “SLA has a boring name for what I mean: *pushed output*. You only notice the sentence you cannot finish when a person is waiting.”

## 5. `easy-korean-reading`

**Draft claim:** stop translating novels; graded readers; if you know most words you’re in the right book; finishing pages is the habit.

| Source | What it actually says | Use in the post |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Hu, M., & Nation, P. (2000). Unknown vocabulary density and reading comprehension. *Reading in a Foreign Language*, 13(1), 403–430. | Pleasure reading without help needs ~**98%** known running words. | “If you know almost none, you are studying vocabulary with extra steps.” |
| Nation, I. S. P. (2006). How large a vocabulary is needed for reading and listening? *Canadian Modern Language Review*, 63(1), 59–82. | **98%** coverage of novels ≈ **8,000–9,000 word families** (English data). Newspapers ~8,000. | A beginner novel is statistically the wrong object. |
| Schmitt, N., Jiang, X., & Grabe, W. (2011). The percentage of words known in a text and reading comprehension. *Language Learning*, 61(S1). PDF: [lextutor](https://www.lextutor.ca/cover/papers/schmitt_etal_2011.pdf) | Comprehension rises with coverage; 98% is the serious threshold. | Don’t pretend 80% guessing is reading. |
| Day, R. R., & Bamford, J. (2002). Top ten principles for teaching extensive reading. *Reading in a Foreign Language*, 14(2). [PDF](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~readfl/rfl/October2002/day/day.pdf) | Principle 1: **the reading material is easy.** Also: read a lot, for pleasure, silent, own choice. | Graded reader is the implementation, not a children’s insult. |
| Nation, I. S. P. (2013 with Anthony). Mid-frequency readers. | Bridging books exist because authentic text’s 98% bar is brutal. | “Drop a level until you turn the page.” |

**Caveat:** Coverage numbers are from **English** corpora. Korean doesn’t have the same word-family lists, but the **density** argument (unknown word every 50 words vs every 5) still holds. Don’t quote “8,000 Korean word families” as if Nation measured Hangul.

**Line you can add:** “Reading research is blunt: you need to already know about 98% of the words on the page to read, not to decode. A thick novel at month four is a dictionary with a plot.”

## Cross-cutting (use once, not in every post)

1. **Four strands (Nation 2007)** — conversation, reading, textbook, Anki, Duolingo all fail in different corners of the same pie.
2. **Output vs input (Swain vs Krashen)** — how-long, conversation, Duolingo.
3. **FSI hours** — how-long + is-korean-hard only. Don’t paste 2,200 into the Anki post.
4. **Declarative → procedural (DeKeyser)** — Anki/textbook/Duolingo store facts; conversation under time pressure is the practice that turns them into speech. One theory name for four posts.

| Jiang et al. later Duolingo papers / white papers (Spanish, French, English; some now include speaking) | Still **not Korean**. In-house or affiliated. | You can mention they eventually tested speaking in other languages. **Do not** import those ACTFL labels onto 듀오링고 한국어. |

**Still no Duolingo Korean speaking RCT.** Streaks-as-habit: cite Lally (cue + daily) carefully — Duolingo is good at the cue, bad at the skill.

**Line:** “A whole systematic review of Duolingo research found that scholars mostly studied the game design, in English, in the US. Korean speaking is not in that pile. That absence is data.”
