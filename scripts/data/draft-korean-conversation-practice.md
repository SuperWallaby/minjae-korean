# How to practice Korean conversation if you're not in Korea

I've done real conversation practice on r/language_exchange and on HelloTalk. Both can work. Both can waste your time. Here's what I actually saw.

## I did practice there — that part is true

People ask how to speak Korean if you're not in Korea.

I'm not guessing. I used r/language_exchange. I used HelloTalk for years. I also tried AI voice tools. Gemini. ChatGPT voice. Some custom talking bot. And later, a private tutor.

So yes — you can get actual practice on Reddit and HelloTalk. Native English speakers especially. Koreans looking for English show up a lot. I found partners. We talked. That counts.

The problem is not "apps don't work."

The problem is finding someone who is there to learn.

## They tend to play each other

A lot of language exchange is not a classroom. It's people in their twenties looking for a friend. Or a vibe. Or something that feels like dating with a language label on it.

HelloTalk especially. Sometimes it felt like a dating app. Photos first. Soft chat. Flirting. Study second — if at all.

I'm not saying every match is like that. I'm saying enough of them are that you burn evenings.

They play each other. Not always on purpose. One person wants Korean. The other wants English and a crush. Or neither wants to correct anything hard. Soft English. Soft Korean. Soft nothing.

That is not serious language learning. It is social.

And a good educational partner — someone who stays in Korean when you stumble, who makes you finish the sentence, who comes back next week — is really hard to find.

You can scroll for hours. Many faces. Few teachers in disguise.

There is no study that proves "HelloTalk always fails" or "a paid tutor always wins." That part is my field note. The research below is about *what kind of talk* actually teaches.

## Conversation is not shadowing

Speaking alone is fine. Narrate your room. Shadow a YouTube clip. Record a voice note.

That is rehearsal. Rehearsal is allowed.

Studies on shadowing — [Hamada (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168815598295), for example — say it helps listening and pronunciation. Useful. Still not a conversation. Nobody is waiting for your answer. Nobody makes you repair it.

Conversation is different. Someone is waiting. You have to answer in time. You get stuck. They ask again. You repair.

Merrill Swain called that pushed output. The short version: you only notice the sentence you can't finish when a person is waiting.

And research on [corrective feedback](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x) (Li, 2010) finds a medium effect that holds over time. Soft chat that never interrupts skips that job.

Shadowing will not give you that. AI voice often won't either. It smiles. It keeps going. It doesn't care if you froze.

## If you are shy

Shy is not lazy. Some people freeze with strangers. That is normal. Research going back to [Horwitz et al. (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4781.1986.tb05256.x) treats speaking anxiety as something you can measure — not a personality insult.

Then a paid tutor can help — not magic, just a regular lesson where someone is there to make you talk. [MacIntyre and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4781.1998.tb05543.x) call this willingness to communicate: readiness to speak with *this* person, in *this* moment. A weekly lesson is a low-threat slot on purpose.

Do not pick the cheapest smile. If they never make you say it again, you bought a friend with a lesson title. A tutor who prompts you to fix the sentence is doing the useful kind of feedback — not being mean.

## What I tell people to do

Keep it small.

> One live conversation a week. Twenty minutes is enough. The same day, if you can.

Before the call, pick one situation. Not "Korean." How was your weekend. What did you eat. Why are you tired. Write five ugly sentences.

After the call, write down two things you could not say. That is tomorrow's study. Not a new textbook chapter.

If you have nobody, that is logistics. Not talent. Reddit and HelloTalk can still work — I did practice there — but hunt like you're hiring. Ask for Korean half the time. Ask them to correct you. Drop the flirty ones fast.

## The other days

The other six days, talk anyway. 물 마시고 있어요. Silly is fine.

Just do not confuse rehearsal with the real thing.

Pair it with a [study plan that has rest days](/blog/article/korean-study-plans-realistic-flexible-goals). A conversation you dread every night dies in two weeks.

## What I would not do

I would not wait until my grammar is ready. It will never feel ready.

I would not memorize 100 travel phrases and call it conversation.

I would not spend a month comparing tutor platforms. One weekly person beats a perfect spreadsheet.

**If you spoke badly to someone this week, that was the practice.**

This is from my experience. You may have had a different one. If you don't agree with me, you're absolutely right.
